# Keep memos, develop themes and record reflexivity

Guide sections: 8
Target length: 4 min
Starting state: practice-study after coded

Recording note: Begin with the three-source, two-code practice study from section 3, without changes from other tutorial recordings.

1. Select `Memos`, then `New memo`. — "Keep your interpretation beside the evidence."
2. Enter `Support and pressure` in `Title`; choose `Analytic` in `Kind`. — "Analytic memos hold interpretations, questions and exceptions."
3. Enter `How does help from others change the experience of pressure?` in `Memo`. — "A memo can hold a question rather than a finished conclusion."
4. Choose `Support` in `Linked code`, `p03` in `Linked case` and its source in `Linked source`. — "Link the memo to the evidence and concepts it concerns."
5. Choose the friends response in `Linked segment`; select `Save memo`. — "Memos can link to a segment, code, case and source."
6. Select `Edit memo`; append `Look for cases where support does not reduce pressure.` to `Memo`. — "Revise your interpretation as you read more closely."
7. Select `Save memo`, then expand `Earlier versions` with its displayed count. — "Earlier memo versions remain available with their replacement times."
8. Select `New memo`; enter `My assumptions` in `Title` and choose `Reflexive` in `Kind`. — "Reflexive memos record how your position shapes what you notice."
9. Enter `I may notice helpful relationships more readily than unresolved stress.` in `Memo`. — "Describe the assumptions that may affect your interpretation."
10. Choose `p01` in `Linked case` and its source in `Linked source`; select `Save memo`. — "Link a reflexive note to the case or source it concerns."
11. Set `Show` to `Reflexive memos`, then return it to `All memo kinds`. — "Use the kind filter to review one part of your memo record."
12. Select `New memo`; enter `Unit of comparison` in `Title` and choose `Method` in `Kind`. — "Method memos record decisions about sampling, coding and analysis."
13. Enter `This practice study compares participants as cases.` in `Memo`; select `Save memo`. — "Make the study's unit of analysis explicit."
14. Select `Codebook`; under `Create code`, enter `Responding to pressure` in `Name`. — "A theme is an interpreted pattern relevant to the research question."
15. Enter `A provisional pattern connecting experienced demands and help from others.` in `Definition`. — "Develop the pattern through interpretation rather than frequency alone."
16. Leave `Parent code` at `No parent` and `Status` at `Active`; select `Save draft code`. — "A parent code can organize the codes supporting a theme."
17. Select `Edit Support`; set `Parent code` to `Responding to pressure`; select `Save draft code`. — "Place a supporting code beneath the proposed theme."
18. Select `Edit Pressure`; set `Parent code` to `Responding to pressure`; select `Save draft code`. — "Hierarchy organizes definitions without rolling child counts into the parent."
19. Select `Freeze codebook`, then return to `Memos` and select `New memo`. — "Freeze the revised draft when its definitions are ready for use."
20. Enter `Responding to pressure: provisional theme` in `Title`; choose `Theme` in `Kind`. — "A theme memo explains the pattern and why it matters."
21. Enter `Help and pressure meet in these accounts; the short walk offers another response.` in `Memo`. — "Describe where the proposed pattern holds and where it does not."
22. Choose `Responding to pressure` in `Linked code`; select `Save memo`. — "Link the theme memo to its parent code."
23. Select `Retrieval`; set `Code` to `Support` and `Case` to `All cases`. — "Retrieve the supporting passages across cases."
24. Read the excerpt cards and their codebook versions, then set `Code` to `Pressure`. — "Retrieval shows current coded spans with their historical frozen versions."
25. Select `Open in transcript` on a matching excerpt. — "Return to the transcript to check the surrounding context."
26. Select `Matrix` and inspect the Support and Pressure rows. — "Compare the evidence across cases before settling on a theme."
27. Select `Analysis` and inspect `Code co-occurrence`. — "Co-occurrence can guide reading, but it does not establish a theme."
